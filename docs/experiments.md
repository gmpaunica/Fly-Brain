## FB-06 fixed-controller baseline — 2026-09-21

The unchanged, hand-wired 100-neuron synthetic network and hand-written motor
decoder were evaluated without Pygame or training. The [machine-readable
results](../experiments/fb06-fixed-baseline/results.json) contain every initial
pose, episode result, config, seed, software/device version, and summary. The
[CSV](../experiments/fb06-fixed-baseline/episodes.csv) has one row per evaluated
episode. `git_commit` is null because this workspace has no Git repository.

The fly succeeds when its **center** enters a radius of 20 world units around
the odor source. Success at the initial pose is allowed. Otherwise an episode
ends on the first successful post-movement tick or at 600 ticks (12 seconds).
Wall contact counts as a collision but does not end an episode. Source,
network, sensory encoding, and motor decoding match the neural demo. Cases
start 60–180 units from the source. Train seeds 0–7 are reserved for future
optimization, validation seeds 100–103 for development, and final seeds
200–207 for a held-out audit. They are disjoint and generated by local seeded
random streams. FB-07 must not tune on final outcomes.

The primary objective is food-arrival rate on unseen starts. Mean normalized
progress uses **all** cases, including timeouts: `min(1, (initial distance -
final distance) / (initial distance - success radius))`. It can be negative
when the fly ends farther from food. Success time and path efficiency have a
success-only denominator; they are null when there are no successes. Path
length uses actual post-clamp movement, and collision count includes each
attempted boundary crossing. These choices are measurement scaffolding.

| Split | Success | Mean progress | Mean final distance | Mean path length | Mean wall contacts |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Validation | 0/4 | -1.688 | 305.3 | 416.1 | 378.3 |
| Final | 1/8 | -3.021 | 393.8 | 468.0 | 261.9 |

The one successful final episode reached food at 2.28 seconds, with path
efficiency 0.977. The small samples do not estimate reliable performance.
Most episodes moved substantially, ended farther from food, and spent many
ticks at a wall. This confirms poor navigation on these starts. The user's
live circling observation has not been reproduced as a sustained loop in this
recorded set: total absolute turn was below 1.4 radians in each case. The
existing tonic forward drive also remains present with zero odor, while the
controller has no programmed search or turn response to zero input; the
2026-09-21 diagnostic in `PROJECT_LOG.md` measured forward output about 0.714
and zero turn after 20 zero-odor decisions. That is an observed control
limitation, not a learned failure mode. There is no training or improvement
claim in FB-06.

## FB-07 supervised spike-readout attempt — 2026-09-21

The [recorded result](../experiments/fb07-readout/results.json),
[episode CSV](../experiments/fb07-readout/episodes.csv),
[loss CSV](../experiments/fb07-readout/curve.csv),
[curve](../experiments/fb07-readout/curve.svg), and
[checkpoint](../experiments/fb07-readout/readout.pt) document one predeclared
training run. The fixed 100-neuron synthetic topology and current encoder were
unchanged. A 7-input, 2-output PyTorch motor readout with bias received EMA
filtered group spike counts. Only its 16 scalars were trainable. The training
target was a hand-written sensory-only rule: forward 0.7 and turn
`clip(8*(right-left)/max(right+left,1e-9),-1,1)`. This is supervised imitation,
not reward learning or learning inside the spiking graph.

Eight locked train starts supplied 200 fixed-controller ticks each: 1,600
samples. Full-batch Adam ran for 80 steps at learning rate 0.05. Mean squared
teacher error fell from 0.104135 to 0.079003. On the first backward pass, all
16 gradients were finite and nonzero (smallest absolute value 0.01918); all
16 parameters changed (smallest absolute net change 0.000125). The topology
hash stayed equal, and a freshly loaded checkpoint exactly replayed the first
validation episode in the recorded CPU environment.

| Split | Learned arrivals | Fixed arrivals | Learned mean progress | Fixed mean progress |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Validation | 0/4 | 0/4 | -1.286 | -1.688 |
| Final | 0/8 | 1/8 | -1.188 | -3.021 |

The primary arrival metric did **not** improve: it tied on validation and
worsened by one success on final. All learned cases timed out. All-case mean
progress improved, but remained negative. These 12 cases are too few for a
reliable population estimate. The result is a failed embodied navigation
attempt despite a real optimizer update; M6 remains open.

An input-aliasing check gives a concrete limitation: the eight train starts
have **one unique first-tick seven-feature vector** but **eight distinct
teacher turn targets**. Their raw antenna concentrations differ, yet the
current encoder saturates above 0.025 and sends identical spike features to
the readout. No deterministic function of that first-tick vector can assign
eight different turns. This explains a specific information bottleneck; it
does not prove that saturation alone caused every timeout. The next task is a
focused sensory observability diagnosis before another training attempt.

Reproduce in the recorded Python/Torch environment with the FB-07 command in
the README. It overwrites FB-07 artifacts. A second run added aliasing metadata
without changing configuration and reproduced the same episode outcomes; no
hyperparameter was selected from final cases. Full method:
[FB-07 plan](plans/M6-LEARNING.md).

## FB-07D sensory observability diagnosis — 2026-09-21

The [diagnostic result](../experiments/fb07d-observability/results.json),
[validation rows](../experiments/fb07d-observability/validation.csv),
[loss curve](../experiments/fb07d-observability/curve.csv), and
[checkpoint](../experiments/fb07d-observability/readout.pt) record one controlled
encoder-scale intervention. The original `2*min(concentration/0.025,1)` current
was compared with `2*min(concentration/0.05,1)`. This doubles only the scale;
the current ceiling, synthetic topology, seven group features, readout size,
teacher, train starts, optimizer, and evaluation protocol remain fixed. The
scale is hand-written scaffolding, not a trainable or sourced biological value.

The paired probe replayed exactly the same pre-movement observations from 200
ticks on each train trajectory and 600 on each validation trajectory. An exact
feature collision means one seven-element filtered spike vector arose from at
least two different raw bilateral readings; conflicting teacher-turn ticks
count vectors whose target turns differ. These counts diagnose the readout's
available information on these trajectories, not the cause of every timeout.

| Fixed-controller observations | Original scale | Wider scale |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Train aliased ticks / 1,600 | 1,030 | 452 |
| Train unique feature vectors | 629 | 1,250 |
| Train ticks with both antennae saturated | 891 | 425 |
| Train minimum turn MSE from exact collisions | 0.134511 | 0.009461 |
| Validation aliased ticks / 2,400 | 1,446 | 1,589 |
| Validation unique feature vectors | 1,019 | 860 |
| Validation ticks with both antennae saturated | 527 | 310 |
| Validation minimum turn MSE from exact collisions | 0.072211 | 0.070585 |

Every aliased tick in this probe also belonged to a vector with conflicting
teacher turns. The MSE floor predicts each exact feature bucket with its mean
teacher turn, then averages squared turn errors across ticks. It is the
irreducible **turn-only** error for a deterministic readout of these features;
actual linear-readout error can be higher. Wider encoding helped on train
observations but reduced feature variety on validation observations. The
validation error floor changed little despite more collisions, so collision
count alone is not a performance measure. Weak concentrations failing to elicit
spikes after scaling are a plausible contributor, not an isolated cause. A
controlled first-tick example demonstrates the narrower
claim: raw readings `(0.03,0.04)` and `(0.04,0.03)` both encode as current
`(2,2)` and produce sensory group spike totals `(10,10)` at scale 0.025. At
scale 0.05, their currents differ and spike totals become `(0,10)` and
`(10,0)`. The JSON records both cases and the actual currents.

One matched readout was then trained on 1,600 train ticks from the wider
encoder's fixed-controller trajectories. Eighty full-batch Adam steps at 0.05
reduced teacher MSE from 0.121692 to 0.079266. All 16 readout scalars had
nonzero first-step gradients and changed; the graph hash stayed fixed, and a
reloaded checkpoint exactly replayed the first validation episode. The
original FB-07 learned and FB-06 fixed validation results are included as
case-level comparisons:

| Case | Wider outcome / progress | Original learned progress | Fixed progress |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| validation-100 | timeout / -2.366 | -1.734 | -1.576 |
| validation-101 | timeout / -1.171 | -1.368 | -1.348 |
| validation-102 | timeout / -0.798 | -0.994 | -1.635 |
| validation-103 | timeout / 0.064 | -1.048 | -2.193 |

Arrival remains 0/4 for all three controllers. All-case mean progress is
-1.068 for wider, -1.286 for original learned, and -1.688 for fixed, but one
case worsened and the sample is tiny. No new final cases were evaluated. The
data supports a specific saturation bottleneck on train observations and a
remaining low-signal observability problem on validation. It does not justify
another readout training run under the current encoding. The next task should
test an encoding that preserves low-concentration differences and spikes on
both train and validation before any more optimization. See
[FB-07D plan](plans/FB-07D.md).

For a concrete PyTorch trace, two concentrations become a 100-element current
tensor: each ten-neuron sensory group receives one scalar current. The frozen
`W[post,pre]` matrix has shape `(100,100)` and acts on a 100-element prior-spike
vector. Counts from seven groups are averaged and filtered to shape `(7,)`.
The learned weight matrix has shape `(2,7)`; `Wx+b` gives forward and turn
before bounding. Only those 16 readout scalars receive autograd gradients and
Adam updates. Changing the encoder alters forward simulation, not the
gradient path through a hard spike threshold.

large readings. Its constants are assumed model units, not biological values
or fitted parameters. The graph, world, recorded observations, 20-substep
timing, seven-group EMA and case boundaries were fixed. The FB-07D train and

reversal are both silent in the first 20 ms under the two linear encoders.
The rational currents are about `(1.458,1.765)`; the sensory group spike

train MSE/activity criteria, but failed train aliasing. No readout training,
embodied evaluation, or new final-case evaluation was run. M6 navigation
learning remains unverified. Next, locate where the remaining collisions
arise along current, spikes, group means and EMA before choosing another
representation. See [FB-07E plan](plans/FB-07E.md).

PyTorch still holds a 100-element current vector, a fixed `(100,100)` weight
matrix and a seven-element filtered feature vector. The rational rule changes
